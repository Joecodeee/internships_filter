# Resources & Tips for Pre-College Internships

## 📚 About This Guide

This guide will help you navigate the world of high school internships, summer programs, and STEM opportunities. These resources can boost your college applications and help discover your interests!

---

## 🎯 Program Tier System

### Tier 1: ELITE (Highly Competitive) 🏆
These programs are selective - great if you get in, but don't be disappointed if you don't!

- **RSI** (MIT Research Science Institute) - Only ~75 US students/year!
- **TASP** (Telluride Summer Program) - Free but very selective
- **Summer Science Program** - Extremely competitive  
- **HMMT** (Harvard-MIT Math Tournament) - For math lovers

**What they want:** You need good grades, test scores, strong essays, recommendations  
**If you get in:** Major college application boost ⭐⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Tier 2: COMPETITIVE (Medium Challenge) 👍
Good programs that want engaged students but aren't impossible

- **iD Tech Summer Camps** - Popular, good availability
- **Code 2040** - Selective but welcomes diversity
- **STEM bootcamps** - Various

**What they want:** Interest in the field, decent grades, commitment  
**If you get in:** Solid college resume addition ⭐⭐⭐⭐

### Tier 3: ACCESSIBLE (Good Chances) ✅
Great programs with higher acceptance rates

- **Science Olympiad** - Join your school team
- **TASC** - Tech competition
- **Junior Achievement** - Broad participation
- **Year Up** - Looking for diverse participants

**What they want:** Interest and willingness to participate  
**If you get in:** Real experience and skills ⭐⭐⭐

### Tier 4: WIDELY AVAILABLE (Easy Access) 🚀
Many opportunities, usually first-come-first-served

- **Local internships**
- **General summer jobs**
- **Online programs**
- **Community programs**

**What they want:** Basic interest and availability  
**If you get in:** Experience and money ⭐⭐

## 📋 Application Basics

### The Resume
**What to Include:**
- Your name, email, phone number at top
- School name and graduation year
- GPA (if 3.5+) or don't include
- Activities/clubs you participate in
- Any volunteer work or jobs
- Skills (coding, languages, etc)
- Awards if impressive
- Keep it ONE PAGE

**Template:**
```
YOUR NAME
Email@gmail.com | (555) 123-4567 | City, State

EDUCATION
High School Name, Graduating 2024/2025
GPA: 3.8 | Activities: Science Club, Debate Team, Volunteer Tutoring

SKILLS
Code: Python, JavaScript, HTML/CSS
Languages: Spanish (fluent), Mandarin (learning)
Other: Design, Leadership, Teaching

EXPERIENCE
Volunteer Tutor at [Organization], 2023-Present
- Helped 5+ students improve grades in math
- Taught concepts clearly and patiently

AWARDS
- Science Fair Winner, 2023
- National Merit Scholar
```

### The Essay
**Common Prompt:** "Why do you want to participate in this program?"

**What to Write:**
1. Real reason you want this (passion/interest)
2. What you hope to learn
3. How it connects to your goals
4. Why YOU specifically (not generic)
5. Closing sentence with enthusiasm

**Example:**
```
I'm applying to iD Tech because I've been coding in Python for 
a year and know this is my passion. I want to build real projects 
and learn game development from professionals. I've taught myself 
basics through free online courses, but I need structured learning 
and mentorship to advance. This program would accelerate my learning 
and help me understand career paths in tech. I'm excited to meet 
other students who share my passion.
```

### The Recommendation
**Who to ask:**
- A teacher who knows you well
- Someone you've worked with closely  
- Someone who can speak to your abilities
- NOT a family member

**When to ask:**
- Early (by October for summer programs)
- In person if possible
- Give them 2+ weeks notice
- Provide the deadline

**What to tell them:**
- What the program is
- Why you want to go
- Due date
- Link to submit if online

**Example message:**
```
Hi Mr. [Teacher],

I know you well through [class/club], and I really respect 
your [teaching style/leadership]. I'm applying to [Program] 
and they ask for teacher recommendations. I'm very serious 
about this opportunity. Would you be willing to write me a 
recommendation? The deadline is [DATE], so I'm looking to 
gather these by [1-2 weeks before]. 

Here's the link if it's an online form: [link]

Thank you so much! Let me know if you need anything.
```

## 💰 Cost Breakdown

### FREE Programs 🎉
- TASP (Telluride Summer Program) - Fully funded
- Code 2040 - Free + stipend
- Kode With Klossy - Free for eligible girls
- Some Science Olympiad activities
- Year Up - Free + sometimes paid

### PAID Programs (You Earn Money!) 💵
- Year Up Youth Program - $500-1500+ for summer  
- Urban League - Wage varies by role
- Boys & Girls Club - Varies, often paid
- Most summer jobs - Minimum wage+
- Local internships - Often paid (sometimes not)

### FUNDED Programs (They Pay for You!)  🏆
- RSI - Fully covered (room, board, tuition)
- TASP - Room, board, tuition covered
- SSP - Partial scholarships available
- Many college research - Paid + housing

### PAYMENT REQUIRED (You Pay) 💸
- iD Tech - $500-2000+ per week
- Some bootcamps - $200-1000+
- Some campus programs - Tuition fees
- Conferences - Registration fees

**Tip:** Financial need programs! Many offer scholarships if you ask.

## ⏰ Timeline for Success

### September-October: The Golden Window
**What to do:**
- Research programs
- Start applications  
- Ask teachers now for recommendations
- Finalize essays
- Start submitting early programs

**Why it matters:** Rolling admissions mean first applicants get first spots!

### November-December: Finish Strong
**What to do:**
- Submit all applications
- Keep track of deadlines (spreadsheet helps!)
- Follow up on recommendations
- Stay engaged with teachers

### January-April: Waiting Period
**What to do:**
- Check email regularly
- Note response dates
- Be ready to apply to backup programs if needed
- Continue high school (don't slack!)

### May-June: Decisions Time
**What to do:**
- Respond to acceptances quickly
- Decline programs you're not doing
- Confirm participation if needed
- Pay deposits if required
- Start preparing (reading, coding, etc)

### July-August: Program Time!
**What to do:**
- Enjoy the experience!
- Network with students
- Work hard and learn
- Take photos/collect stories
- Get recommendation from supervisor

### September-After: College Application Time
**What to do:**
- Put experience on college applications
- Write about what you learned
- Ask program coordinator for recommendation
- Use experience in essays

## 🌟 How to Stand Out

### In Your Application
✅ Show genuine interest (not generic answers)  
✅ Explain why THIS program (not just any program)  
✅ Share specific goals or projects  
✅ Show initiative (self-taught coding, clubs you started, etc)  
✅ Be authentic, not trying too hard  
❌ Don't copy friends' essays  
❌ Don't exaggerate your experience  

### In Your Resume
✅ Include recent activities/achievements  
✅ Quantify when possible (taught 5 students, built 3 apps)  
✅ Use action verbs (led, created, designed, taught)  
✅ Keep it concise  
❌ Don't make up experience  
❌ Don't list every little thing  

### As a Person
✅ Show passion (genuine interest)  
✅ Show initiative (started projects, sought learning)  
✅ Show respect (ask good questions, listen)  
✅ Show growth mindset (learn from failures)  
✅ Show potential (you don't have to be perfect!)

## 🎓 College Application Benefits

These programs impress colleges because:

1. **Selective Programs = Rigor** → Colleges see you challenge yourself
2. **Real Experience** → Not just classroom learning
3. **Initiative** → You sought out these opportunities
4. **Commitment** → You spent weeks/months on it
5. **Growth** → You leveled up your skills
6. **Passion** → Shows real interest in field

**Best for college apps:**
- RSI, TASP, Summer Science Program
- Paid internships
- Leadership roles
- Research experience
- Projects you completed

## 🆘 Dealing with Rejection

**If you don't get accepted:**

1. **Don't panic** - There are 56+ opportunities here!
2. **Apply elsewhere** - You have fallback options
3. **Ask for feedback** - Some programs tell you why (helpful!)
4. **Try again next year** - You can reapply
5. **Do something else** - A local internship is valuable too!
6. **Learn** - Each rejection teaches something

**Reality check:** Even selective applicants don't get in everywhere. Getting rejected means nothing about your worth!

## 🏆 How to Maximize the Experience

Once accepted:

### Before It Starts
- Read all prep materials
- Study subjects if recommended  
- Get necessary tools (laptop, etc)
- Connect with other students on social media if available

### During the Program
- Show up on time
- Work hard
- Ask questions
- Help classmates
- Network with instructors
- Challenge yourself
- Take notes/photos

### After It Ends  
- Stay in touch with other students
- Connect with instructors on LinkedIn
- Document what you learned
- Write reflection on experience
- Get recommendation/certificate
